Technical Guide

How cloud-mask-composite-images turns an area of interest and a time range into a cloud-free satellite composite: the steps it runs, how it masks clouds and builds the composite, how the GeoTIFF is encoded, and how the map overlay is rendered.

Overview

The workflow is a Python port of the GEE-PICX approach. In outline:

  1. Read the area of interest (ROI) from a vector file, repair its geometries and merge every feature into one shape.
  2. On Google Earth Engine, find the Sentinel-2 or Landsat scenes that overlap the ROI in the time range and are under the scene cloud-cover threshold.
  3. Mask clouds, cloud shadow and cirrus pixel by pixel in each scene, using the sensor's own quality band.
  4. Reduce the remaining clear pixels over time to one value per pixel (median by default). Add any spectral indices and a valid_count band.
  5. Download the composite tile by tile and write it to one GeoTIFF in the results folder.
  6. Colour one band (or a true/false-colour combination), lay it over the base maps, and show it on the dashboard.

Unlike GEE-PICX, which exports to Google Drive in the background, this workflow downloads the composite while it runs. The GeoTIFF is in the results folder when the run finishes.

Scene selection and cloud masking

Cloud cover is handled at two levels:

  • Per scene. Scenes whose cloud cover is above Cloud Threshold are left out completely.
  • Per pixel. In the scenes that are kept, pixels flagged as cloud, shadow or cirrus by the quality band are masked. They do not count towards the composite.

A higher threshold keeps more scenes. Their cloudy pixels are still masked, so in persistently cloudy regions a higher threshold usually gives better coverage.

The time range is applied as [since, until): until itself is excluded.

Sentinel-2

CollectionCOPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED (Level-2A surface reflectance)
Scene filterCLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE ≤ threshold
Pixel maskScene Classification (SCL). Only classes 4 vegetation, 5 bare soil, 6 water, 7 unclassified and 11 snow/ice are kept. Cloud shadow, medium/high-probability cloud, cirrus, dark-area and saturated/defective pixels are masked.
BandsB2 → blue, B3 → green, B4 → red, B8 → nir, B11 → swir1, B12 → swir2
ReflectanceDN / 10000
Default pixel size10 m (B11/B12 are natively 20 m and are resampled)

Landsat

Landsat 5, 7, 8 and 9 (Collection 2, Tier 1, Level-2) are merged into one collection. Whichever satellites were flying during the time range contribute scenes.

CollectionsLANDSAT/LT05/C02/T1_L2, LE07, LC08, LC09
Scene filterCLOUD_COVER ≤ threshold
Pixel maskQA_PIXEL bits 1–4 (dilated cloud, cirrus, cloud, cloud shadow) must all be 0. Pixels with any saturated band (QA_RADSAT ≠ 0) are also masked.
BandsL5/L7: SR_B1–B5, SR_B7. L8/L9: SR_B2–B7. Both are renamed blue, green, red, nir, swir1, swir2.
ReflectanceDN × 0.0000275 − 0.2
Default pixel size30 m

Landsat 7 scenes after May 2003 have scan-line gaps (SLC-off). With several scenes in the range the median usually fills them. A short range that only has Landsat 7 will show stripes.

Compositing

After masking, each band is reduced over time pixel by pixel with the chosen Reducer:

ReducerUse it for
median (default)General use. Robust to cloud edges and haze the mask missed.
meanSmoother results when the masking is clean. Sensitive to missed clouds.
maxPeak greenness when NDVI is the main output, but bright missed clouds win in the visible bands.
minDarkest observation. Often picks up cloud shadow the mask missed.

Each band is reduced on its own, so one output pixel can take its red and its NIR from different dates.

The valid_count band counts the clear observations behind each pixel. It is the number of unmasked red values in the range. Use it to judge how far to trust a pixel: 1 means a single image, 0 means no clear observation, and the pixel is empty in every other band.

Spectral indices

Indices are computed from the composite bands (surface reflectance, 0–1) after reduction, not per scene. A median NDVI here is the NDVI of the median red and NIR. That is not the same as the median of each scene's NDVI.

IndexExpressionMeasures
NDVI(nir − red) / (nir + red)Vegetation greenness
EVI2.5 (nir − red) / (nir + 6 red − 7.5 blue + 1)Greenness, less saturated over dense canopy
SAVI1.5 (nir − red) / (nir + red + 0.5)Greenness corrected for soil background
MSAVI(2 nir + 1 − √((2 nir + 1)² − 8 (nir − red))) / 2As SAVI, with no tuning factor
NDMI(nir − swir1) / (nir + swir1)Vegetation moisture
NDWI(green − nir) / (green + nir)Open water
NBR(nir − swir2) / (nir + swir2)Burn scars (compare before/after)
NDBI(swir1 − nir) / (swir1 + nir)Built-up areas
NDSI(green − swir1) / (green + swir1)Snow
BSI((swir1 + red) − (nir + blue)) / ((swir1 + red) + (nir + blue))Bare soil

Indices use all six reflectance bands from the composite, even when those bands are not selected under Bands. Bands only controls which reflectance bands are written to the file.

The GeoTIFF

One file per run, written to ECOSCOPE_WORKFLOWS_RESULTS:

composite_<sensor>_<roi-hash>_<since>_<until>.tif
e.g. composite_sentinel2_3f9a1c2e_2026-09-01_2026-09-30.tif

The ROI hash is the first 8 characters of the MD5 of the merged ROI geometry, so different areas never overwrite each other. Running the same ROI, sensor and dates again replaces the file.

Band orderThe selected Bands, then the selected Indices, then valid_count. Each band's description is set to its name.
Data typeint16
ScalingReflectance and index bands are stored × 10 000 (divide by 10 000 to read them). Values are clamped to ±32 767. valid_count is not scaled.
No data-32768 in every band outside the ROI, and in reflectance/index bands where valid_count is 0.
CRSThe UTM zone of the ROI's centre, e.g. EPSG:32736.
GridThe ROI's bounding box, expanded outward to a multiple of the pixel size.
LayoutTiled 256 × 256, DEFLATE compression, BigTIFF when needed.

To use it in QGIS or Python, divide by 10 000 and set -32768 as no data. For example, with rasterio:

import rasterio
with rasterio.open(path) as src:
    i = src.descriptions.index("NDVI") + 1
    ndvi = src.read(i, masked=True) / 10_000

Downloading from Earth Engine

The composite is fetched with ee.data.computePixels, one square tile at a time, and each tile is written straight into the GeoTIFF. Earth Engine rejects responses over about 48 MB, so tiles are sized to keep each response under 32 MB (and at most 2048 px on a side):

tile edge = min(2048, √(32 000 000 / (bands × 2)))

With the default 6 bands + NDVI + valid_count (8 bands), a tile is 1414 px, about 14 km on a side at 10 m. Download time grows with the area of the ROI's bounding box:

Bounding boxSentinel-2 (10 m)Landsat (30 m)
10 × 10 km1 tile1 tile
50 × 50 km16 tiles4 tiles
100 × 100 km64 tiles, ~1.6 GB before compression9 tiles

The whole bounding box is downloaded, including the parts outside the ROI, which come back as no data. A long, diagonal ROI costs as much as its bounding box. Progress is printed as tile n/N written.

Rendering the map overlay

A 10 m composite is too large to draw as polygons, so composite_to_bitmap_layer turns it into one image:

  1. Read the chosen band (or the three bands of true_color = red/green/blue, false_color = nir/red/green) and divide by 10 000.
  2. Reproject to Web Mercator (EPSG:3857), average-resampled down so the long side is at most 2048 px. A 100 km ROI is drawn at about 50 m per pixel. The GeoTIFF keeps full resolution.
  3. Stretch the colour scale from the 2nd to the 98th percentile of the values in the ROI. RGB composites are stretched channel by channel.
  4. Colour with the Palette, or the band's default below. No-data pixels are transparent.
  5. Embed the result as a PNG in a bitmap layer at the chosen Opacity. Single bands get a six-step legend. RGB composites have no legend.

Recompiling

spec.yaml is the source. ecoscope-workflows-cloud-mask-composite-images-workflow/ is generated from it by wt-compiler.

./dev/recompile.sh --update   # keeps the lock file and bumps VERSION.yaml
./dev/recompile.sh --install  # a new lock file, and the packages installed

Testing

Each case in test-cases.yaml runs the workflow with its settings:

pixi run --manifest-path pixi.toml -e default bash dev/pytest-cli.sh cloud_mask_composite_images --all

The test cases call Earth Engine, so they need valid GEE credentials and network access.
